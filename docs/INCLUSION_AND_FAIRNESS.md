# Inclusion, gender and fairness

## Distinct constructs

Gender identity, transgender experience, sexual orientation, partner preferences, relationship status, and relationship intent are distinct. A transgender woman is a woman; a transgender man is a man. Nonbinary, gender-fluid and multi-label identities must be represented without forcing false binary categories. Asexual-spectrum individuals may have romantic interests; asexuality is not equivalent to being unavailable for dating.

## Preferences, not stereotypes

Do not derive a deterministic attraction rule from labels such as gay, straight, bisexual, pansexual, queer, nonbinary or transgender. Ask optional, consent-based questions about who an individual might consider. Never infer sensitive characteristics from names, appearance, geographic proxies or public profiles. Unknown preferences remain unknown, not incompatible.

## Reciprocity

Both people's voluntarily expressed age and partner preferences matter. A reference person's age never creates an automatic upper age cutoff. A user may apply an explicit adult age filter, but filtering must be shown separately from evidence-based acceptance probability. Do not equate group-average willingness to consider an age difference with attraction to a particular individual.

## Population counting

Use disjoint unique-person records or cells. Adding `women + transgender people + nonbinary people` double-counts overlapping categories. If published tables offer only overlapping marginals, use statistical bounds or explicitly modeled joint distributions with appropriate uncertainty.

## Fairness review

Audit coverage and calibration across supported groups; assess measurement invariance, selection bias, nonresponse and subgroup effective sample sizes. Do not rank groups by desirability or introduce arbitrary identity penalties. Publish suppressed or aggregated results for small groups. Participation and disclosure must remain optional.
