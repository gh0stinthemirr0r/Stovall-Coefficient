# Ethics, consent and privacy

## Scope

The Stovall Coefficient describes potential **aggregate dating opportunity**; it cannot establish consent, entitlement to attention, availability of an identifiable person, or an individual's probability of accepting contact. Only adults 18+ are in scope.

## Data minimization

Prefer publicly available aggregate counts and voluntarily supplied non-identifying survey information. Do not scrape dating profiles, infer sexual orientation or transgender status, or construct lists of identifiable prospects. Do not retain names, precise addresses, contact details, or unneeded sensitive attributes.

## Consent and governance

Obtain informed, revocable consent for any primary research; permit skipping sensitive questions; define retention and deletion; restrict access; encrypt research records; conduct ethics review where applicable. Public releases should contain synthetic or disclosure-safe aggregate data only.

## Reidentification

Small intersections of geography, age, gender identity and orientation can identify people. Suppress small cells, avoid fine-grained geographic maps of sensitive identities, and assess reconstruction risk across linked releases. Differential privacy may be useful but requires a formal privacy budget and an analysis of accuracy loss.

## Misuse controls

Do not use the coefficient to target unwilling individuals, justify discrimination, rank protected groups, or imply that probability equates to consent. Include warnings in any user-facing application. Provide uncertainty and missing-data explanations prominently.
